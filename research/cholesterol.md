# Diet, LDL-cholesterol and cardiovascular risk: evidence brief

Compiled 2026-11-07 by a research agent. Direct fetches of nice.org.uk, pubmed and heartuk.org.uk were blocked from the session, so NICE and Heart UK wording is confirmed via secondary summaries and flagged where relevant. Verify any number you send to someone else against the primary source.

## 1. What the major guidelines say about diet

**ESC/EAS 2025 Focused Update** (Mach et al., Eur Heart J 2025). Reaffirms the 2019 diet advice: saturated fat under 10% of energy (under 7% if LDL raised), replace with mono- and polyunsaturated fat, 25 to 40 g fibre, 2 or more fish servings a week. New points: no dietary supplement or vitamin is endorsed for LDL lowering; Lp(a) should be measured at least once in adult life; plant sterols/stanols (about 2 g/day) "may be considered" in people not yet needing drugs.

**AHA 2026 Dietary Guidance** (Circulation, March 2026, supersedes 2021). Nine pattern features: energy balance for a healthy weight; plenty of vegetables and fruit; wholegrains; healthy protein sources (plants, fish, low-fat dairy, lean meat); food sources of unsaturated fat in place of saturated fat (nuts, seeds, avocado, non-tropical liquid oils); minimally processed over ultra-processed; minimise added sugars; limit salt; limit or avoid alcohol. The AHA 2019 advisory on dietary cholesterol remains: no numeric egg cap, but dietary cholesterol raises LDL dose-dependently (about 1.9 to 4.6 mg/dL per 100 mg/day), and eggs are reasonable within a low-saturated-fat pattern.

**NICE NG238 (Dec 2023, replaced CG181)**. Risk via QRISK3 for ages 25 to 84. Offer atorvastatin 20 mg if 10-year risk is 10% or more after shared decision-making; statins can be considered below 10% on informed preference. Full non-fasting lipid profile (total, HDL, non-HDL, triglycerides). Diet (secondary-source wording): total fat 30% of energy or less, saturated fat 7% or less, dietary cholesterol under 300 mg/day, replace saturated with mono- and polyunsaturated fat (olive, rapeseed), 5 or more portions fruit and veg, 2 or more portions fish a week (one oily), 4 to 5 portions unsalted nuts, seeds or legumes a week, wholegrain starches. NICE does not advise plant sterols/stanols or omega-3 supplements for CVD prevention (lack of outcome evidence, not lack of LDL effect).

**Heart UK UCLP** (Ultimate Cholesterol Lowering Plan). Step 1 motivation; step 2 heart-healthy foundation (cut saturated fat, swap to unsaturated, fibre, fruit and veg, weight); step 3 add the four cholesterol-lowering foods: soya foods, oat or barley beta-glucan (3 g/day or more), tree nuts and peanuts (about 30 g/day), sterol/stanol-fortified foods (1.5 to 2.4 g/day). Each lowers LDL by about 3 to 10%; effects described as additive. Developed with an Alpro educational grant (declared).

**Where they disagree**: NICE does not recommend sterols/stanols, ESC says "may be considered", Heart UK promotes them. Everyone agrees on the core: cut saturated fat, replace it with unsaturated fat (not refined carbohydrate), more fibre, no fish-oil capsules for prevention.

## 2. Quantified LDL reductions (meta-analyses)

| Component | Dose | LDL effect | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oat beta-glucan | 3 g/day or more | -0.19 to -0.27 mmol/L (about 5 to 7%) | Whitehead 2014 (28 RCTs); Ho 2016; Yu 2022 |
| Psyllium | about 10 g/day, 3 weeks or more | -0.33 mmol/L (95% CI -0.38 to -0.27) | Jovanovski 2018, AJCN, 28 RCTs |
| Plant sterols/stanols | 2.0 to 2.5 g/day | -8.4% (7.6 to 9.2) | Ras 2014, Br J Nutr, 124 studies |
| Soy protein | about 25 g/day | -3 to -4% (-0.12 mmol/L) | Blanco Mejia 2019, J Nutr, 46 RCTs |
| Nuts | 28 g/day | -0.12 mmol/L; larger at 60 g/day or more | Del Gobbo 2015, AJCN, 61 trials |
| Portfolio diet (all the above combined, low saturated fat) | pattern | -17% (-0.73 mmol/L) in meta-analysis; about -29% in 4-week metabolic feeding (comparable to lovastatin 20 mg); about -13% free-living at 6 months | Chiavaroli 2018, 7 trials; Jenkins 2003, 2011 |
| Replace 1% of energy from saturated fat with polyunsaturated | per 1% | roughly -0.04 to -0.055 mmol/L; monounsaturated about -0.03 | Mensink 2016, WHO regression, about 84 trials |
| Reduce saturated fat, hard outcomes | | CVD events RR 0.83 (0.70 to 0.98), 12 trials, n=53,758; no effect on mortality; benefit tracks the size of the cholesterol fall | Hooper 2020 Cochrane |
| Mediterranean diet | pattern | Small LDL effect. Benefit is on events: PREDIMED about 30% fewer major CV events in primary prevention; CORDIOPREV HR about 0.72 to 0.75 vs low-fat in secondary prevention | Estruch 2018; Delgado-Lista 2022 |

Practical reading: UCLP-style stacking (saturated fat swap + oats + nuts + sterols + soy) realistically gives a 10 to 20% LDL reduction free-living; 25 to 30% needs near-total adherence. Statins give 35 to 50%.

## 3. Weight loss itself

- Hasan 2020 (JCEM, 73 RCTs, n about 32,500), per kg lost: triglycerides -0.045 mmol/L, LDL -0.033 mmol/L, HDL +0.012 mmol/L. So 10 kg is roughly LDL -0.33, triglycerides -0.45, HDL +0.12 mmol/L.
- Blood pressure, Neter 2003 (25 RCTs): about -1.05 systolic and -0.92 diastolic mmHg per kg.
- Look AHEAD (Wing 2011): 5 to 10% loss gives meaningful improvements in BP, HbA1c, triglycerides and HDL; LDL benefit only clear at 10% or more.
- For him: expect triglycerides and BP to move more than LDL from weight loss alone. Diet composition is what moves LDL.

## 4. Specific controversies (2023 to 2026)

- **Coconut oil**: raises LDL by about +0.27 mmol/L vs non-tropical vegetable oils (Neelakantan 2020, 16 trials). Not a health food.
- **Butter vs olive oil**: Zhang 2025 (JAMA Intern Med, 221,054 US health professionals, observational): replacing 10 g/day butter with plant oil associated with about 17% lower mortality. RCTs show the LDL mechanism. Swap to olive or rapeseed.
- **Red and processed meat**: weak evidence linking unprocessed red meat to ischaemic heart disease (Burden of Proof 2022); processed meat associations are stronger and consistent. Limit processed meat firmly; lean unprocessed meat in moderation.
- **Ultra-processed food**: Lane 2024 BMJ umbrella review: CVD mortality RR 1.50 but very low certainty. Dicken 2025 Nature Medicine RCT (n=55, 8 weeks, both diets Eatwell-compliant): minimally processed diet produced about 1% more weight loss. Directionally sound, effect sizes uncertain.
- **Sugar and triglycerides**: higher sugars raise triglycerides and LDL modestly (Te Morenga 2014); fructose raises triglycerides mainly in calorie excess. Matters mainly via calories and weight.
- **Alcohol**: the J-curve has largely collapsed. Biddinger 2022 (UK Biobank Mendelian randomisation, n=371k): risk rises at all intakes. AHA 2025 statement: do not recommend alcohol for heart health. Alcohol raises triglycerides and BP directly. UK CMO: 14 units a week or less, spread over 3 or more days. Less is better; no protective dose.
- **Fish oil supplements**: REDUCE-IT (4 g icosapent ethyl, positive, mineral-oil comparator criticised) vs STRENGTH (4 g EPA+DHA, null). Atrial fibrillation risk increased about 25% overall and about 50% at over 1 g/day (Gencer 2021). NICE and ESC 2025: no over-the-counter omega-3 for prevention. Eat oily fish; skip capsules.
- **Red yeast rice**: monacolin K is lovastatin. EFSA 2018 and 2025: cannot identify a safe intake. Kobayashi (Japan 2024): 5 confirmed deaths and over 100 hospitalised, with many more deaths under investigation, from a contaminated batch. An unregulated statin with variable dose. Avoid.
- **Eggs**: about +2 mg/dL LDL per 100 mg cholesterol, with wide individual variation. Up to about one a day is acceptable if saturated fat is low; the problem is usually the bacon and butter around them.

## 5. What to ask the GP for (England)

1. NHS Health Check (free, age 40 to 74 without CVD, diabetes or CKD, every 5 years): height, weight, waist, BP, cholesterol, diabetes risk, alcohol, smoking, activity, QRISK.
2. Full non-fasting lipid profile: total, HDL, non-HDL, triglycerides, LDL.
3. Lp(a), once. Heart UK 2019 consensus and ESC/EAS 2025 recommend it. Not routine; the GP may need to request it specifically, or it can be done privately for roughly 50 to 95 pounds. Heart UK threshold of concern is over 90 nmol/L.
4. QRISK3 as a number, not "fine". NICE offers atorvastatin 20 mg at 10% or more. BMI 30 and age 50 can push a man near this even with average lipids.
5. HbA1c, BP (ideally home readings too), kidney function (feeds QRISK3).
6. Ask whether non-HDL and QRISK would change meaningfully with 10 kg loss, to frame a diet-first trial.

## 6. Time course and when to retest

- LDL reaches a new steady state about 2 to 4 weeks after a sustained change in fat, sterol or fibre intake. Weight-loss-related changes continue as weight falls.
- Retest at 8 to 12 weeks to capture both the direct dietary effect and early weight loss. Use the same lab; non-fasting is fine. Single readings vary 5 to 10% biologically, so do not over-read small moves.

## Key caveats

- Mortality benefit from saturated fat reduction is not shown in RCTs (Hooper 2020); event benefit is moderate certainty.
- Portfolio and UCLP effect sizes come from small trials, some industry-involved.
- UPF, butter-mortality and fish-oil-harm findings are observational.
- NICE wording verified via secondary summaries only.
