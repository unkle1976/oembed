# Running plan

Built from `research/running.md`. You ran marathons once. Your heart and lungs will remember within weeks. Your Achilles, shins, plantar fascia and knees are at beginner capacity and are carrying 10 to 15 kg more than they did. The 2025 study of 5,000 runners and 588,000 sessions found the thing that predicts injury is a single run much longer than anything in the previous month, not weekly mileage. So the whole plan is: never spike.

## Before you start

- Book the GP check (see `plan/gp-checklist.md`). You do not have to wait for it to start walk-run at an easy pace; current ACSM screening does not require clearance for moderate exercise in someone with no symptoms and no diagnosed condition. You should wait for it before adding anything hard.
- Stop and seek medical advice if you get: chest, neck, jaw or arm discomfort; breathlessness out of proportion to effort; dizziness or fainting; palpitations; calf pain on walking that eases with rest. Chest pain or fainting during exercise means stop and seek urgent care.
- Two pairs of shoes if you can afford it; rotating pairs was associated with 39 percent fewer injuries in one cohort. Comfort is the only shoe criterion the evidence supports.

## Intensity

Every run in this plan is conversational pace. If you cannot speak in full sentences, slow down or walk. Ignore heart-rate zones for now; age formulas are unreliable at 50 and you do not need them yet. Easy running gives you the same fat loss as hard running at lower injury cost and higher adherence.

## The schedule

Three sessions a week, never on consecutive days (48 hours between runs lets tendon and bone recover while fitness still builds). Every session starts and ends with 5 minutes of brisk walking.

| Week | Each of 3 sessions | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8 x (60 s run / 90 s walk) | 8 min |
| 2 | 6 x (90 s run / 2 min walk) | 9 min |
| 3 | 2 x (90 s run / 90 s walk, then 3 min run / 3 min walk) | 9 min |
| 4 | 3 min run, 90 s walk, 5 min run, 2.5 min walk, 3 min run, 90 s walk, 5 min run | 16 min |
| 5 | Repeat week 4. Deliberate consolidation because you are heavier than a typical starter | 16 min |
| 6 | 5 min run, 3 min walk, 5 min run, 3 min walk, 5 min run | 15 min |
| 7 | 8 min run, 5 min walk, 8 min run | 16 min |
| 8 | 10 min run, 3 min walk, 10 min run | 20 min |
| 9 | 25 min continuous (sessions 1 and 2), 20 min (session 3) | 20 to 25 min |
| 10 | 28, 25, 30 min continuous | 25 to 30 min |

This is the NHS Couch to 5K structure with one extra consolidation week. The NHS app will talk you through it if you want a voice in your ear; just repeat week 4.

## Repeat-a-week rules

Repeat the week, do not advance, if any of these happened:
- You could not hold a conversation during the run intervals.
- Any pain above 3 out of 10 during a run or the next morning.
- Morning stiffness in the Achilles, shin or heel that lasts beyond the first few steps.
- You missed a session.

Never skip a week. Repeating is normal; the NHS plan is built for it.

## After week 10

Hold 3 x 30 minutes for 3 to 4 weeks. Then one change at a time: add a fourth easy run, or lengthen one run by no more than 10 percent of your longest run in the past month. Nothing faster than conversational pace until you have been running continuously for 3 months and the GP check is clear.

## Niggles

A localised pain that gets worse during a run, is there the next morning, or changes how you walk is a stop signal. Two easy days, then repeat the week. The most common injuries in your situation are Achilles tendinopathy, shin pain, kneecap pain and plantar fasciitis. Calf strength (see `plan/strength-plan.md`) is the best-evidenced protection against the first two.

## Recovery

Sleep 7 to 9 hours. Short sleep raises injury risk and appetite. Watch for overreaching: resting heart rate drifting up, heavy legs for several days, worse sleep, irritability, recurring colds. If it lasts over a fortnight, cut volume and see the GP.

## What to expect

- VO2max up 10 to 15 percent over 12 weeks, typically one fitness category.
- Systolic blood pressure down about 3 to 4 mmHg from the training alone, more if it is currently high, plus about 1 mmHg per kilo lost.
- Resting heart rate down, more if it starts high.
- The first 150 minutes a week of moderate activity buy most of the mortality benefit in the big cohorts. Three 30-minute sessions plus the walking warm-ups gets you there by week 10.
- Fitness matters more than the scales. In a 2025 meta-analysis of nearly 400,000 people, fit men with obesity had no significantly higher mortality than fit normal-weight men, while unfit men in every weight category had 2 to 3 times the risk.
