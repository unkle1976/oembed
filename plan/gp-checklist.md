# GP checklist

Built from `research/cholesterol.md` section 5 and `research/nutrients.md` section 8. Book this in the first fortnight. You are worried about heart disease and stroke, and the honest answer is that nobody can tell you your risk without these numbers. They also give a baseline so the diet can be shown to work.

## Ask for

1. **NHS Health Check.** Free, 40 to 74, every 5 years in England, at the GP or some pharmacies. Covers height, weight, waist, BP, cholesterol, diabetes risk, QRISK.
2. **Full lipid profile**, non-fasting is fine: total, HDL, non-HDL, LDL, triglycerides.
3. **Lp(a), once in a lifetime.** Genetic, does not change with diet, and raises risk independently. ESC 2025 recommends it for every adult once; Heart UK recommends it. Not routine on the NHS, so ask directly. If refused, it is available privately for roughly 50 to 95 pounds. Over 90 nmol/L is Heart UK's threshold of concern.
4. **QRISK3 as a number.** Not "you're fine". NICE offers atorvastatin 20 mg at 10 percent or more over 10 years. At 50 with BMI 30 you may be close to that even with ordinary lipids.
5. **HbA1c.** BMI 30 puts you at diabetes risk. Under 42 normal, 42 to 47 prediabetes, 48 or over diabetes (mmol/mol).
6. **Liver function (ALT, AST, GGT).** Fatty liver affects up to 1 in 5 UK adults and is more common in men at BMI 30. It reverses with weight loss, so it is worth knowing.
7. **Kidney function (U&E, eGFR).** Feeds QRISK3, and worth having before starting creatine or a potassium-based salt substitute.
8. **Full blood count, B12 and folate, thyroid (TSH).** Reasonable after a year of poor diet.
9. **Blood pressure**, and ask about home readings if the clinic reading is borderline.

Vitamin D is usually not tested routinely and the GP will say to just take 10 ug a day. That is correct advice.

## Questions to ask

- "What is my QRISK3 number, and roughly how much would it fall if I lost 10 kg and brought my non-HDL down 20 percent?" This frames a diet-first trial with a date to review.
- "Is there anything in these results that means I should not start a walk-run programme and two gym sessions a week?"
- "Can we retest lipids and liver at 12 weeks?"

## Tell them

- You are starting 1600 kcal a day, a walk-run programme and strength training.
- You plan to take vitamin D and creatine (creatine can nudge the creatinine reading up; it is not kidney damage).
- Any family history of heart attack or stroke before 60.
